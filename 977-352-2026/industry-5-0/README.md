# The Industrial Revolution: From Industry 1.0 to Industry 5.0

## Overview
The **Industrial Revolution** refers to a series of major transformations in production, technology, and society that fundamentally changed how goods are produced, how people work, and how economies function. These revolutions are commonly categorized into **five stages**, each driven by key technological breakthroughs.

---

## Industry 1.0 – Mechanization (≈ 1760–1840)

### 🔧 Key Driver
- Steam power and mechanical machines

### 🏭 Characteristics
- Manual labor replaced by machines  
- Birth of factories  
- Expansion of textile and mining industries  

### 🌍 Impact
- Shift from agrarian to industrial society  
- Urbanization and factory-based work  

**Representative technology:** Steam engine  

---

## Industry 2.0 – Electrification & Mass Production (≈ 1870–1914)

### ⚡ Key Driver
- Electricity and assembly-line manufacturing

### 🏭 Characteristics
- Mass production and standardization  
- Division of labor  
- Faster and cheaper manufacturing  

### 🌍 Impact
- Rise of global trade  
- Growth of large corporations  

**Representative concept:** Assembly-line manufacturing  

---

## Industry 3.0 – Automation & Digitalization (≈ 1970–2000)

### 💻 Key Driver
- Electronics, computers, and automation

### 🏭 Characteristics
- Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs)  
- Computer Numerical Control (CNC)  
- Early industrial robotics  

### 🌍 Impact
- Reduced human intervention  
- Increased precision and productivity  

**Representative technology:** PLC-based automation  

---

## Industry 4.0 – Smart & Connected Industry (≈ 2010–Present)

### 🌐 Key Driver
- Cyber–Physical Systems (CPS), IoT, AI, Big Data

### 🏭 Characteristics
- Smart factories  
- Real-time monitoring and optimization  
- Digital twins and predictive maintenance  

### 🌍 Impact
- Data-driven decision making  
- Flexible and intelligent manufacturing  

**Representative concept:** Smart factory  

---

## Industry 5.0 – Human-Centric & Sustainable Industry (Emerging)

### 🤝 Key Driver
- Human–machine collaboration

### 🏭 Characteristics
- Collaborative robots (cobots)  
- Human-in-the-loop control  
- Focus on sustainability and resilience  

### 🌍 Impact
- Personalized production  
- Ethical, inclusive, and sustainable industry  

Industry 5.0 is strongly promoted by the **European Commission** to align industrial development with social and environmental values.

**Representative idea:** Humans + AI working together  

---

## Comparison Summary

| Revolution | Power Source | Key Technology | Role of Humans |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|---------------|
| Industry 1.0 | Steam | Mechanical machines | Manual labor |
| Industry 2.0 | Electricity | Assembly lines | Operators |
| Industry 3.0 | Electronics | PLCs, CNC | Supervisors |
| Industry 4.0 | Data & Internet | CPS, IoT, AI | Decision-makers |
| Industry 5.0 | Human intelligence | Cobots, CPS | Central collaborators |

---

## Link to Society 5.0
- **Industry 4.0** focuses on technology and efficiency  
- **Industry 5.0** aligns industrial systems with human well-being  

Together, **Industry 5.0 supports Society 5.0**, a vision originally proposed by the Government of Japan, where technology is designed to serve society as a whole.

---

## One-Paragraph Textbook Summary
The industrial revolution has evolved through five major stages, from steam-powered mechanization to human-centric intelligent manufacturing. While Industry 1.0–3.0 emphasized mechanization and automation, Industry 4.0 introduced cyber–physical integration and data-driven intelligence. The emerging Industry 5.0 highlights sustainability, resilience, and close collaboration between humans and intelligent machines, marking a shift toward value-driven and socially responsible industrial development.
